# External Use Evidence Guide

## Purpose

The project welcomes genuine outside use and keeps external-adoption claims traceable to
verifiable evidence.

## Strong forms of evidence

Examples include:

- a public paper, preprint, thesis, poster, report, or syllabus citing the Atlas;
- a derivative repository that imports or extends the package;
- an external pull request;
- a reproducible issue based on real technical use;
- an independent replication;
- a documented classroom or research-lab use case;
- a public organizational demonstration; or
- a letter that accurately describes actual organizational use.

## Evidence record

For each genuine external use, record:

- user/organization or public project name;
- date;
- component used;
- nature of use;
- public URL or document, when available;
- whether the use was independent of the maintainer; and
- any technical or methodological feedback.

## What is not evidence

Do not manufacture or inflate:

- stars;
- forks;
- users;
- citations;
- testimonials;
- download claims; or
- organizational adoption.

A page view or star may be a useful engagement signal, but it should not be presented as
substantive adoption by itself.

## Privacy

Do not publish private business records, applicant data, personal information, internal
emails, or confidential agreements merely to document use.
